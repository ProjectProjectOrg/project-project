## Usage

```tsx
<div className="bg-card shadow-(--shadow-1)" />          {/* a card in a dense row */}
<div className={cn("relative bg-card", avatarEdgeClass)} /> {/* an object: avatar, tile */}
<Popover.Popup className={popupSurfaceClass} />         {/* every popup */}
<div className="bg-surface-1"><div className="bg-surface-3" /></div> {/* nested */}
```

| | What it is | Where |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tone | A step of lightness (`--page`, `--card`) | Every surface, first |
| `--shadow-1` | A 1px ring, no offset, no blur | Board cards, dense rows |
| Lit edge (`avatarEdgeClass`) | A 1px edge plus a soft light along the top | Avatars, project tiles, attachments, embeds |
| `popupSurfaceClass` | The tooltip's raised fill (`--tooltip`) and edge (`--tooltip-edge`), 19px corners | Popovers, menus, selects, the slash menu |
| `--surface-1` … `8` | A ladder of layers, with their own ring | Surfaces on surfaces (board columns and cards) |
| `.button-fill`, `.pp-badge` | A sheen from the fill's own colour | Filled buttons, badges, chips |

## Rules

### Flat by default

Pages, panels, cards and rows are flat. Depth comes from tone (a lighter or darker step) and, where tone isn't enough, a hairline. Drop shadows on resting rectangles are what makes a tool look busy (and what the apps this one is designed against do everywhere), so there are none.

### A hairline is not a shadow

`--shadow-1` draws a 1px ring with no offset and no blur. It separates without lifting, and since it's a shadow rather than a border, it takes no space, so a dense row doesn't shift by a pixel. It's allowed at rest.

### Objects get a lit edge

Things that are objects rather than areas (an avatar, a project tile, an attachment, a Figma embed) get the lit edge: a hairline in a tenth of the text colour plus a soft light along the top. It reads as a physical thing catching light, in both themes, without a shadow under it.

### Popups share one surface

Every popup is `popupSurfaceClass`: the tooltip's raised skin, a lifted fill with a hairline and a light along the top, nothing else. Popovers, menus, hover cards and tooltips all wear it, so whatever floats reads as one family. The tonal step and the hairline already separate it from the page; a shadow would only add noise to a menu that's gone in a second. A popup opened from inside another (a select in a popover) steps one level up, to `--tooltip-raised`, so the two layers never share a fill.

### Both themes layer the same way

The ladder steps down for a recessed well and up for a card in light and in dark, by comparable amounts. Light's upward steps are smaller (white is a ceiling), but never flat, so a nested card stays visible. One deliberate exception: settings groups are white cards in light, because a grey well on the light page reads muddy.

### Sheen, not gradients

Filled buttons and badges carry a faint vertical sheen mixed from their own colour (`color-mix(in oklab, fill, white n%)`), shown at rest, gone on hover, a little stronger on press. It gives a fill some material without a gradient that would shift its colour.

### Don't nest cards

A card inside a card means one of them is wrong; use the ladder or drop a level.
