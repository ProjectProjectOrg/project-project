## Usage

The primitives do all of this themselves. When building a new interactive element:

```tsx
<button
  className={cn(
    "focus-ring transition-[background-color,scale] duration-150 ease-standard",
    "not-disabled:hover:bg-hover not-disabled:active:scale-[0.97]",
    "disabled:cursor-not-allowed disabled:opacity-50"
  )}
/>
```

## Rules

### Hover only on things that act

A static element never changes on hover; a hover says "this does something". Hover is a see-through overlay (`--hover`), so it works on any surface, lands instantly and fades out ([Motion](/foundations/motion)).

### Press: darker and smaller

Pressed deepens the overlay (`--active`) and scales the control to 97%. Both, because either alone is easy to miss: the scale is felt, the tone is seen.

### Focus: a grey ring, off the edge

Keyboard focus is the `focus-ring` class: a 2px `--ring` outline 2px outside the control, following its corners. Grey, not a brand colour (there isn't one), at 3:1 against every surface. It shows only for keyboard focus, never animates, and doesn't move on press, so it stays a steady "you are here". No glows, no coloured box-shadow rings.

### Disabled steps back, and says why

Disabled is about half opacity (45–50%) with `cursor-not-allowed`, and hover and press are off (`not-disabled:`), so it never pretends to respond. A control the viewer's role can never use isn't shown at all; disabled is for "not now" (a pending save, a closed sprint), and the reason sits next to it or in its tooltip.

### Loading keeps its shape

A loading control stays at full strength with `cursor-progress` and swaps its label for the logo loader at the same size, so nothing around it moves.

### Hit areas of 24px or more

Every target is at least 24×24 (WCAG 2.5.8). Small visual controls (a 16px checkbox, a 12px chip action) extend their hit area with a pseudo-element or a 24px round target, so they stay small to look at and easy to hit.

## Fields and menus step from their surface

A field, its hover and focus, and the menu highlight are worked out from the surface they sit on (`--surface`), a fixed step of lightness away, so they read the same on the page, a settings group and a popup. `--surface` is the page; a surface that holds fields declares its own with the `surface` class (`surface [--surface:var(--card)]`), as `popupSurfaceClass` and settings groups do, and the tokens are worked out again inside it.

```css
/* dark */
--field:       oklch(from var(--surface) calc(min(l - 0.04, 0.23)) c h);
--field-line:  oklch(from var(--surface) calc(l + 0.04) c h); /* field edge */
--field-hover: oklch(from var(--field)   calc(min(l + 0.06, 0.26)) c h);
--field-focus: oklch(from var(--field)   calc(l + 0.025) c h);
--menu-hover:  oklch(from var(--surface) calc(l + 0.06) c h);
--menu-active: oklch(from var(--surface) calc(l + 0.10) c h);
/* light: fields stay white (min(1, l + 0.02)), states step darker */
```

A field's edge steps from the surface too: a white overlay on the field would land on the surface's own lightness and vanish. In dark a field never gets lighter than 0.23, so on a raised surface (a nested popup) it stays a well instead of drifting up to grey with its surface, and its hover stops at 0.26 so it stays below the popup's fill. Light can't get lighter than white, so a focused field there is carried by the focus ring, not a tint.

Row and control hover (`--hover`, `--active`) stay translucent overlays: they stack, so a chip still lights up on a hovered row, and they work on coloured fills.
