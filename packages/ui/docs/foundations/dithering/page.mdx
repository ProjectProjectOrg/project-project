## Usage

You rarely draw dithering yourself; the components that carry it do:

```tsx
<Empty>…</Empty>                          {/* a dithered well, seeded */}
<MemberAvatar member={member} size={24} /> {/* a photo, or a generated orb */}
<ProjectIcon seed={project.id} color={project.color} size={28} />
<Logo />
```

The tokens: `--dither-front` and `--dither-back` (the two greys), and `--dither-front-subtle` / `--dither-front-strong` for the empty state's intensities.

## Rules

### Two languages that never compete

The interface is clean and type-driven (buttons, inputs, rows, menus in neutral greys); the graphics are dithered. Keeping them apart is what lets both work: the chrome stays quiet enough to disappear under the work, and the dithering stays rare enough to be recognisable as the brand.

### Never on a control

Dithering on a button, a row or a field would make it noisy and harder to read, and would spend the signature on something people look through, not at. The one dithered button (`variant="dither"`) is for marketing and hero moments; the app itself doesn't use it.

### Generated, never drawn per screen

Empty states use a dithered well with a random seed and an icon tile, never a custom illustration, so no two look alike and no screen needs artwork. Members without a photo get an orb seeded by their id; projects get a tile seeded by theirs. The same seed always paints the same picture, so a person or a project is recognisable at a glance.

### Colour stays the user's

The mark and empty states are grey. Avatars and tiles take colour only from the user's palette ([Colour](/foundations/colour)): a project's chosen colour, or three tag-wheel colours for a person, translucent over the surface so they read as pastels in light and deep tones in dark.

### Stepped, not smooth

Ordered dithering on a 4×4 Bayer matrix, at device pixels, so the pattern stays crisp at any zoom: a soft gradient would look like any other app; the stepped one is ours.
